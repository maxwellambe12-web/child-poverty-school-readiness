**Child Low Income and School Readiness: Cheshire West and Chester vs England**

An analysis of two public health "wider determinants" indicators from the OHID Public Health Outcomes Framework, comparing Cheshire West and Chester (CW&C) with England and with other English local authorities.



The question

- How has child low income in CW&C changed compared with England between 2021/22 and 2024/25?
- How does CW&C compare with other English local authorities in 2024/25 on child low income and on school readiness?
- Is there an observable association between the two across local authorities?

Key findings

- Child low income: CW&C was 15.1% in 2024/25, below the England rate of 19.8%, and has stayed below England throughout 2021/22 to 2024/25.
- School readiness (children eligible for free school meals): CW&C was 45.0% in 2024/25, below the England rate of 51.3%.
- The two point in different directions for CW&C: comparatively low child poverty, but comparatively low school readiness among disadvantaged children.
- Association across local authorities: very weak. Area-level child low income does not explain much of the variation in school readiness among FSM-eligible children, so other local factors are likely to matter.

Indicators

| Code | Indicator |
|------|-----------|
| B01b | Children in absolute low income families (under 16s) |
| B02a | School readiness: children achieving a good level of development at the end of Reception (free school meal-eligible children) |

Method

1. Extracted indicator data for CW&C, England and all English local authorities from the OHID Public Health Outcomes Framework.
2. Built trend, ranking and comparison tables in Excel.
3. Examined the relationship between the two indicators across local authorities, using a scatter chart and trendline.
4. Wrote up the findings and limitations in a PDF report.
5. Built a Power BI dashboard summarising the trend and the local authority comparison.

Limitations

- This is an area-level analysis. It describes local authorities, not individual children or families, and does not show causation.
- The readiness figures cover only children eligible for free school meals, not all children.
- A few areas (for example City of London and Isles of Scilly) have very small cohorts, which produces extreme and unstable percentages.
- 2024/25 figures may be revised in later publications.
